# Station for Humanity — Social Contract Draft

The social layer is intended to be visible to participants and treated as infrastructure rather than marketing.

## Participant rights direction

A mature Station for Humanity should aim to provide participants with:

1. clear rules and disclosed fees;
2. understandable accounting of created value;
3. visibility into public fund flows;
4. meaningful human appeal for consequential automated decisions;
5. data portability and privacy controls;
6. recognition of verified contribution;
7. access to retraining and opportunity when technology changes work;
8. transparent dispute processes;
9. protection against hidden rule changes and arbitrary economic exclusion.

## Social mechanisms

### Dream Fund
Supports verified human goals through transparent plans, milestones and evidence of execution.

### Builder & Pension Pool
Long-term protection for people whose sustained contribution helped build the station. The final mechanism must be financially sustainable and legally reviewed.

### Opportunity Fund
Education, retraining, tools and access that help people move to new roles as technology evolves.

### Emergency Fund
A transparent resilience mechanism for verified participants facing genuine crisis.

## No One Becomes Obsolete

The network cannot guarantee that every profession or company survives technological change. It can design for a different promise:

> A profession may become obsolete. A person should have a path toward another role, skill and source of dignity and income.

## Governance direction

Future governance may include representation for builders, contributors, beneficiaries / dream participants, business partners and independent audit or ethics functions.

No founder, investor, government, corporation or AI provider should automatically receive unlimited authority over the entire network.

## Transparency boundary

Public transparency does not mean publishing private personal data. The project should distinguish between:

- public rules and fund accounting;
- auditable aggregate value flows;
- private participant identity and sensitive information.
