# Station for Humanity — Constitutional Protection Levels (Draft 0.3 working map)

**Status:** proposed classification for Draft 0.4 review. This file does not itself ratify or entrench any article except immutable Article 0.

The purpose is to prevent a future governance majority from treating every constitutional sentence as equally easy to change.

## Level 0 — Immutable

### Article 0 — LIFE FOR LIFE

No amendment process exists.

A system that no longer accepts Article 0 may fork away or become another project, but it cannot amend Article 0 while claiming constitutional continuity with Station for Humanity.

### Proposed amendment protection

Before maturity: founder + independent legal/rights review; no silent changes.

After ratified governance exists, investigate a threshold such as:

- at least **80%** in the Participants & Dreams Assembly;
- at least **75%** in Builders & Contributors Council;
- approval in Partners & Nodes Council where organizational rights are materially affected;
- formal Independent Rights & Audit Board opinion;
- public review period of at least **60 days**;
- second confirmation vote after cooling-off for rights reductions.

A change that **expands** a protected right may use a somewhat lighter process than one that reduces it, but expansion must still be tested for contradictions and unintended harm.

## Level 1B — Anti-capture / integrity guarantees

These articles protect the system itself from permanent capture, provider dependency and unaccountable power.

Proposed articles:

- **11 — Intelligence Creates Responsibility**
- **13 — Security Is a Shared Duty**
- **21 — Planet Is a Stakeholder**
- **23 — AI Is a Participant Class, Not an Automatic Ruler**
- **24 — AI Actions Require Provenance**
- **25 — Political Rights of Non-Human Intelligence Are Not Assumed**
- **26 — No Self-Granted Authority**
- **27 — Provider Independence**
- **29 — Separation of Powers by Function** (principle, not exact body design)
- **30 — Representation Must Follow Affected Interests**
- **31 — Founding Stewardship Is Transitional** (principle, not numerical triggers)
- **32 — Conflicts of Interest Must Be Disclosed**
- **35 — Independent Review Must Become Possible**
- **37 — The Protocol Must Outlive Any One Company**
- **40 — Forkability Is a Last-Resort Anti-Capture Mechanism**

These should use strong constitutional thresholds because weakening them can indirectly destroy Level 1A rights without formally editing those rights.

## Level 2A — Constitutional values with adaptable implementation

The principle is constitutional, but implementation may evolve substantially.

Proposed articles:

- **3 — Equal Rules, Not Artificial Equality of Outcomes**
- **12 — Cooperation Before Conflict**
- **15 — Value Before Money**
- **16 — Reward Follows Real Value**
- **18 — Automation Dividend**
- **19 — Social Infrastructure**
- **22 — Future Generations Have Standing in Design**
- **38 — Federation With a Common Constitutional Floor**

Examples of what can change without abandoning the principle:

- exact social-fund percentages;
- precise eligibility criteria;
- economic formulas;
- federation interfaces;
- environmental accounting methods;
- specific reward mechanisms.

A Level 2 implementation cannot be used to violate Level 1 rights.

---

## Level 2B — Institutional architecture

The following constitutional topics must evolve as the participant base grows:

- exact council names and seat counts;
- term lengths;
- election / sortition / delegation methods;
- voting formulas;
- quorum;
- contributor-weight functions;
- partner voting caps;
- participant uniqueness mechanism;
- founder-transition numerical triggers;
- audit-board appointment process;
- regional node representation;
- emergency renewal mechanics.

Most of this belongs in `GOVERNANCE.md` and future ratified governance rules rather than being frozen into the Constitution too early.

### Proposed amendment process after maturity

Candidate baseline:

- 2/3 of affected representative bodies;
- published impact analysis;
- public review period;
- constitutional compatibility check;
- full version history.

## Meta-constitutional articles

Articles **41–43** define constitutional levels, amendment process and ratification honesty.

These are structurally special: if they are too easy to amend, an attacker can lower the amendment threshold and then remove rights.

Therefore the rules governing how Level 1 protections can be amended should themselves receive **Level 1 protection** after ratification.

---

## Non-regression principle

A lower-level policy cannot override a higher-level rule.

Priority order:

`Article 0 → Level 1A rights → Level 1B integrity rules → Level 2 constitutional values → governance/operational policy → individual decisions`

When two rules conflict, the lower-level rule must be changed, narrowly interpreted or suspended rather than silently overriding the higher-level rule.

---

## Rights-impact test for every constitutional amendment

Every future amendment proposal should explicitly answer:

1. Which participants gain power?
2. Which participants lose power?
3. Which economic rights change?
4. Does the change operate retroactively?
5. Does it increase concentration of control?
6. Does it reduce privacy, appeal or auditability?
7. Does it create a new dependency on one provider or company?
8. What happens if a hostile actor uses this rule exactly as written?
9. Can the change be reversed without harming third parties?
10. Is the same objective achievable with a lower constitutional impact?

A constitutional amendment without an impact analysis should not proceed to binding ratification.

---

## Draft 0.4 unresolved questions

Before ratification, the Station still needs to decide:

- whether Level 1A and Level 1B use identical thresholds;
- whether rights expansion and rights restriction should use different thresholds;
- how to handle conflicts between privacy and audit;
- whether environmental/future-generation standing receives an independent guardian or remains a review duty;
- what evidence could trigger Article 25 review of non-human political rights;
- whether any narrow founder constitutional veto survives the founding transition, and if so for how long / on what exact grounds;
- what legal entities can actually enforce constitutional promises across jurisdictions;
- how a federation node loses constitutional-compatibility status;
- how to prevent amendment fatigue while preserving meaningful public review.

The protection-level map should be attacked in simulation before it is ever ratified. The goal is not to make rules impossible to improve. The goal is to make **rights harder to destroy than ordinary software settings are to change.**
