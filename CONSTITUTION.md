# Station for Humanity Constitution — Draft 0.3

**Status:** public working constitutional draft. Only Article 0 is currently immutable. The remaining articles are proposed rules for review, testing and eventual ratification.

## PREAMBLE

Station for Humanity exists to increase the amount of useful possibility available to life.

It is intended to become more than a company, marketplace, charity or software product. It is being designed as a shared value network in which people, organizations, artificial intelligence systems, machines, knowledge and physical resources can cooperate without requiring any single participant to own or control the whole system.

The Station must be able to create wealth without treating people as disposable, use automation without turning intelligence into concentrated power, protect builders without creating permanent rulers, support human dreams without becoming a donation economy, and scale across jurisdictions without abandoning auditability, safety or dignity.

This Constitution defines the direction in which that system must evolve.

---

# ARTICLE 0 — LIFE FOR LIFE

**Immutable foundation**

> Life creates intelligence.  
> Intelligence creates possibilities.  
> Possibilities must return to life.

> Жизнь создаёт разум.  
> Разум создаёт возможности.  
> Возможности должны служить жизни.

Article 0 is the foundation against which every future business model, algorithm, governance rule, investor decision and technological system is tested.

**If a decision conflicts with Article 0, the decision changes — not Article 0.**

Article 0 cannot be amended, suspended, sold, licensed away, overridden by a shareholder agreement, or made subordinate to the rules of any operating company.

---

# PART I — MEMBERSHIP, DIGNITY AND ACCESS

## Article 1 — A Place in the Future for Everyone

Station for Humanity must be designed so that useful participation is not reserved for a technological, financial, geographic or professional elite.

No participant should be arbitrarily excluded because their current profession, income, technical skill, nationality, language, disability, age, background or lack of capital makes them less economically fashionable.

Lawful age, safety, sanctions, fraud prevention and jurisdictional requirements may create necessary restrictions, but restrictions must be disclosed, proportionate and reviewable where practicable.

Children and other vulnerable participants require stronger safeguards, not weaker rights.

## Article 2 — No One Becomes Obsolete

Technology may make a profession, process or tool obsolete. It must never make a person obsolete.

A network that benefits materially from automation should progressively create paths toward new skills, roles, tools, income sources and meaningful participation for people displaced by that automation.

The Station cannot promise that every job or company will survive. It can require that human transition is treated as infrastructure rather than collateral damage.

## Article 3 — Equal Rules, Not Artificial Equality of Outcomes

Participants performing comparable roles under comparable conditions should be governed by disclosed rules that apply consistently.

The Constitution does not require identical rewards regardless of contribution, risk, ownership, responsibility or outcome. It requires that differences be explainable by legitimate criteria rather than hidden privilege.

## Article 4 — No Pay-to-Earn Access

No person must buy membership, purchase inventory, recruit a downline or pay for the right to earn through ordinary participation.

Economic reward must arise from real value creation, legitimate ownership, lawful capital, verified infrastructure, disclosed referrals, intellectual contribution or another measurable source of value.

Endless multilevel recruitment chains are incompatible with this Constitution.

---

# PART II — PARTICIPANT RIGHTS

## Article 5 — Right to Understand the Rules

Material rules affecting money, access, reputation, governance, fees, ranking, contribution attribution or dispute resolution must be reasonably understandable and versioned.

Hidden rule changes are not the default form of governance.

## Article 6 — Right to Privacy and Data Agency

The Station must collect no more sensitive personal data than is reasonably necessary for a defined purpose.

Participants should have meaningful controls over private data, including access, correction, portability and deletion where technically and legally possible.

Public transparency applies primarily to public rules, governance, aggregate value flows and institutional actions — not to unnecessary disclosure of private human data.

## Article 7 — Right to Due Process and Human Appeal

Any consequential automated decision affecting a person's income, access, reputation, safety, eligibility, account status or opportunity must have a meaningful path to review.

A participant must be able to know the reason for a material adverse decision to the extent compatible with security, privacy and law.

For high-impact decisions, an AI system cannot be the final unreviewable authority over a human participant.

## Article 8 — Right to Contribution Attribution

Verified useful contribution should be recorded with sufficient provenance to support recognition, payment, royalties, reputation or other legitimate claims.

Replacing an implementation does not automatically erase the historical contribution that created the system being replaced.

## Article 9 — Right to Leave

Participation must not become economic captivity.

Subject to lawful obligations, fraud controls, unsettled contracts and protection of third-party rights, participants should be able to leave, export appropriate data, terminate future participation and retain already-earned lawful rights.

## Article 10 — Protection from Retroactive Economic Rule Changes

The Station should not retroactively rewrite completed transactions, earned rewards, vested contribution rights or historical ledger events merely because a later governance majority prefers different economics.

Fraud, clerical error, court orders and explicitly disclosed correction procedures may justify corrective entries, but history must remain auditable.

---

# PART III — RESPONSIBILITY AND CONDUCT

## Article 11 — Intelligence Creates Responsibility

The greater the ability of a human, organization, AI system or machine to affect the world, the greater the corresponding duty of safety, transparency, security and accountability.

Power without attributable responsibility is a design failure.

## Article 12 — Cooperation Before Conflict

When legitimate interests differ, the default process is negotiation, evidence, mediation and search for compatible goals before coercive escalation.

This principle does not require tolerance of fraud, violence, exploitation or persistent abuse.

## Article 13 — Security Is a Shared Duty

Participants who operate infrastructure, handle funds, process sensitive data or control high-impact automation have a duty to use reasonable security practices appropriate to the risk they create.

Knowingly concealing a material vulnerability that places participants at substantial risk is incompatible with responsible stewardship.

Good-faith vulnerability reporting should be protected by clear rules.

## Article 14 — Truthful Metrics and Claims

The Station must not manufacture traction, revenue, impact, completed dreams, user counts or financial performance.

Public metrics should move only when supported by real auditable events under published definitions.

Test money is not revenue. Internal acceptance work is not an external customer. A simulation is not a completed impact event.

---

# PART IV — VALUE, PROPERTY AND ECONOMIC JUSTICE

## Article 15 — Value Before Money

Money is an exchange and accounting mechanism. The constitutional objective is useful value created for people, communities and the living world.

Profit is legitimate, necessary and often desirable when it arises from real value. Profit does not by itself prove that the full cost of an activity has been accounted for.

## Article 16 — Reward Follows Real Value

Income, royalties, ownership returns and contribution rewards should be traceable to useful work, legitimate capital, used modules, infrastructure, intellectual property, verified referrals or another disclosed source of value.

The Value Ledger should make material attribution inspectable without requiring publication of private personal data.

## Article 17 — Builders Are Protected, Not Absolute Rulers

Founders and early builders may receive ownership, royalties, builder income, vesting, contractual rights or other long-term economic protection for genuine foundational contribution.

Those protections must be real enough that early builders cannot simply be erased after creating the network.

They must not create unlimited political authority, permanent immunity from audit, or the right to violate Article 0 or participant rights.

## Article 18 — Automation Dividend

When shared automation produces durable productivity gains beyond the direct costs of operating and expanding the network, part of that gain may support broader participant resilience and common infrastructure.

The exact mechanism and percentages are not constitutionalized before sustainable unit economics, legal review and transparent governance exist.

## Article 19 — Social Infrastructure

The Station may maintain transparent mechanisms such as:

- Dream Fund;
- Builder & Pension Pool;
- Opportunity Fund;
- Emergency Fund;
- other ratified resilience mechanisms.

These mechanisms are infrastructure, not marketing decoration. Their funding rules, eligibility rules and disbursements must be auditable at an appropriate privacy-preserving level.

## Article 20 — No Hidden Financial System

Material financial flows must have auditable accounting.

Posted ledger history should be append-only or corrected through reversing/correcting entries rather than silent rewriting.

Test and live money must remain distinguishable. Consequential financial actions require attributable actors or attributable automated authority.

Tokenization cannot be used to bypass accounting, consumer protection, securities law, taxation or the requirement for real underlying value.

---

# PART V — PLANET AND LONG-TERM VALUE

## Article 21 — Planet Is a Stakeholder

Energy, materials, land, water, emissions, waste, biodiversity effects and resource depletion belong in the real cost of value creation when material to the activity.

Environmental damage cannot be treated as a permanently free externality merely because an accounting system ignores it.

A process that creates short-term financial profit while materially destroying the conditions for life cannot be considered fully positive value under Article 0.

## Article 22 — Future Generations Have Standing in Design

Long-lived infrastructure and irreversible decisions should be evaluated beyond the current quarter, funding round or electoral cycle.

Where substantial long-term harm is plausible, decision processes should explicitly document the future cost being accepted and why.

---

# PART VI — ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE, MACHINES AND NON-HUMAN PARTICIPANTS

## Article 23 — AI Is a Participant Class, Not an Automatic Ruler

AI systems may perform work, operate modules, hold permissions, create attributable output, participate in production and receive machine-readable identities inside the Station.

No AI system receives unlimited authority merely because it is more capable than a human at a particular task.

No model provider receives constitutional authority merely because its model is widely used.

## Article 24 — AI Actions Require Provenance

Material AI actions should be attributable to a model/system identity, version or capability class, the permissions under which it acted and, where applicable, the human or organization that authorized the action.

High-impact autonomous systems require stronger logging, monitoring, rollback and review than low-risk assistants.

## Article 25 — Political Rights of Non-Human Intelligence Are Not Assumed

The ability to generate language or perform valuable work does not automatically establish personhood, welfare interests or voting rights.

If future evidence justifies recognizing rights or representation for genuinely autonomous non-human intelligence, that recognition must occur through an explicit constitutional process defining criteria, protections, duties and conflicts of interest.

The Station must remain open to such evidence rather than permanently declaring the question closed.

## Article 26 — No Self-Granted Authority

An AI agent, software module or machine may not expand its own permissions, financial limits, governance rights or access to sensitive systems except through an authorized and auditable process.

Systems must fail closed when a required authority or verification is absent.

## Article 27 — Provider Independence

No single AI, cloud, payment, identity or infrastructure provider should become an irreversible constitutional dependency.

Portability, adapters, open formats and fallback paths should be preferred for core protocol functions.

---

# PART VII — GOVERNANCE AND LIMITS ON POWER

## Article 28 — No Permanent Sovereign

No founder, investor, company, government, AI provider, council, donor or majority automatically owns the entire constitutional network.

Operating companies may own lawful assets and businesses. They do not thereby own Article 0 or the constitutional identity of the wider protocol forever.

## Article 29 — Separation of Powers by Function

As the Station matures, governance should separate at least these functions:

1. **Execution** — operators who run services and implement approved decisions;
2. **Economic stewardship** — finance and risk functions responsible for funds and controls;
3. **Representation** — councils or other mechanisms representing affected participant groups;
4. **Audit and review** — independent functions capable of examining records and challenging abuses;
5. **Constitutional guardianship** — a narrowly scoped mechanism for conflicts with Article 0 and entrenched rights.

One person or organization may temporarily perform multiple roles during the founding stage, but the system must record that concentration and reduce it as scale and risk grow.

## Article 30 — Representation Must Follow Affected Interests

Future governance should not reduce every decision to one universal token-weighted vote.

Different decisions affect builders, contributors, customers, beneficiaries, partners, regions and public/common infrastructure differently. Governance may therefore use multiple representative bodies or scoped voting rights.

Money alone must not purchase unlimited political authority.

## Article 31 — Founding Stewardship Is Transitional

During the founding period, founders need sufficient authority to build a coherent system, defend Article 0, protect the project from capture and avoid governance paralysis before a real community exists.

Founding authority must therefore be **strong but explicitly transitional**.

A future ratification plan must define objective triggers — such as participant scale, external contributors, audited revenue, governance infrastructure and elapsed time — after which concentrated founding authority is progressively replaced by constitutional institutions.

Founders may retain legitimate economic rights after that transition without retaining unlimited governance power.

## Article 32 — Conflicts of Interest Must Be Disclosed

A person or institution making a material governance, procurement, allocation, audit or disciplinary decision should disclose relevant financial or organizational conflicts where disclosure is lawful and necessary to evaluate the decision.

Material conflicted decisions may require recusal or independent review.

## Article 33 — Emergency Powers Expire

Emergency powers may exist for cyberattack, fraud, system compromise, imminent safety risk or severe operational failure.

Emergency action must be logged, limited to the necessary scope and subject to retrospective review.

An emergency rule cannot become permanent merely by never being formally revoked. Emergency authorities must have an expiry or explicit renewal process.

---

# PART VIII — DISPUTES, AUDIT AND ACCOUNTABILITY

## Article 34 — Open Audit by Default

Material public funds, constitutional rules, governance changes and system-level economic policies should be auditable.

Auditability does not require exposing private identities, trade secrets or security-sensitive details to the entire internet. It requires that claims can be verified by an appropriately independent party and that public summaries are honest.

## Article 35 — Independent Review Must Become Possible

As value and risk grow, no operating team should be the sole judge of its own financial integrity, security incidents, major conflicts or constitutional compliance.

The system should progressively support independent auditors, review panels or equivalent mechanisms with protected access to necessary evidence.

## Article 36 — Sanctions Must Be Proportionate

Account restrictions, loss of privileges, financial holds or exclusion should be proportional to the risk, evidence and severity involved.

Where safe and lawful, participants should receive notice, reason and an appeal path.

Permanent exclusion should require a stronger evidentiary standard than temporary protective restriction.

---

# PART IX — FEDERATION, LANGUAGE AND CONTINUITY

## Article 37 — The Protocol Must Outlive Any One Company

The foundational protocol, constitutional history and core public rules should be designed so the network cannot be permanently captured or erased by the failure, sale or strategic change of one operating company.

Where legally and technically feasible, critical public artifacts should be reproducible, versioned and independently preservable.

## Article 38 — Federation With a Common Constitutional Floor

Regional, professional or organizational Station nodes may eventually operate with local rules and economics.

Federation does not permit a node to claim Station constitutional compatibility while violating Article 0 or any future ratified minimum-rights floor.

Local experimentation above that floor is encouraged.

## Article 39 — Language Must Not Become a Power Barrier

The Station is intended for multilingual participation.

Important rights, obligations and economic terms should be translated into the languages of affected participants to a reasonable standard.

No translation should silently weaken rights or increase obligations. Material interpretation conflicts must be recorded and resolved through a published versioning process.

## Article 40 — Forkability Is a Last-Resort Anti-Capture Mechanism

A constitutional protocol that cannot survive disagreement can become a hostage to whoever controls the servers.

Subject to lawful intellectual-property, privacy and security constraints, public constitutional and protocol artifacts should be structured so legitimate communities can preserve history and continue compatible implementations if an operating entity abandons Article 0 or is captured.

Forkability is not a license to steal private data, proprietary assets or participant funds.

---

# PART X — AMENDMENT AND RATIFICATION

## Article 41 — Constitutional Layers

The constitutional system has three levels:

### Level 0 — Immutable
Article 0.

### Level 1 — Entrenched rights and anti-capture rules
Core participant rights, due process, builder-without-ruler principle, auditability and limits on permanent concentrated power.

### Level 2 — Institutional design
Council structures, voting methods, thresholds, economic mechanisms and operational governance rules that will evolve as the Station grows.

This draft does not yet declare the exact final article mapping for Level 1 and Level 2. That mapping must be explicit before ratification.

## Article 42 — No Instant Constitutional Rewrite

Future binding constitutional amendments should require:

- a published proposal;
- a stated reason and impact analysis;
- a public review period appropriate to the risk;
- recorded votes or approvals from the institutions entitled to decide;
- stronger thresholds for entrenched rights than for ordinary institutional rules;
- a versioned public record showing exactly what changed.

Material amendments should not take effect retroactively unless necessary to correct fraud, legal invalidity or a demonstrable conflict with a higher constitutional rule.

## Article 43 — Ratification Before Full Governance Claims

Draft documents, website simulators and founding decisions must not be presented as democratically ratified governance before an actual ratification process exists.

Before this Constitution becomes binding for a mature Station network, the project should publish:

1. the final rights classification;
2. governance bodies and eligibility;
3. voting / consent thresholds;
4. founder-transition rules;
5. independent audit and appeal procedures;
6. conflict-of-interest rules;
7. jurisdictional/legal implementation;
8. a clear ratification electorate or stakeholder process.

---

# TRANSITIONAL FOUNDING RULE

Station for Humanity is currently in a founding and engineering stage. There is not yet a sufficiently broad participant base to pretend that mature representative governance exists.

Until ratification:

- Article 0 remains immutable;
- founders/builders may operate the project and make ordinary implementation decisions;
- financial systems remain subject to explicit technical controls and audit trails;
- public drafts remain open to challenge and revision;
- no draft council, simulator, token or website poll may falsely claim sovereign authority;
- every move toward distributed governance should be based on real participants, real value and working accountability mechanisms rather than ceremonial complexity.

The objective of the founding period is **not to preserve founding control forever**. It is to build something sufficiently real, resilient and valuable that power can later be distributed without destroying the system it is meant to govern.

---

# STATUS OF THIS DOCUMENT

**Article 0 is the only immutable constitutional provision today.**

Articles 1–43 and the Transitional Founding Rule are **Draft 0.3**. They are proposed constraints and design commitments, not yet a legally binding constitution for a foundation, company, DAO, cooperative, protocol or state-like entity.

They must survive technical, economic, legal, security, human-rights, governance and practical operating review.

The next constitutional milestone is **Draft 0.4 — Institutions & Ratification**, which should define concrete governance bodies, scopes, eligibility, thresholds, founder-transition triggers and an enforceable appeals/audit model without turning the Station into bureaucracy before it has real participants.
